## PART ONE: HOW THE BASELINE VIABILITY ACT DIED IN COMMITTEE

It died on a Tuesday.

This is where everything important dies. Not with drama. Not with a villain's monologue. With a Tuesday.

The bill had survived three readings. It had survived the lobbying from the neural interface consortium, which had argued — correctly, within the framework they operated — that "operational necessity" was too narrow a standard, that competitive environments had competitive requirements, that the market could not be expected to wait for independent certification when milliseconds of cognitive advantage translated directly into survival.

The argument was not wrong about the market. It was wrong about what the market was for.

The amendment that killed the bill was eleven words.

*"Competitive necessity shall constitute sufficient grounds for enhanced integration requirements."*

Eleven words. Introduced at 11:47pm in a session that had been running since 9am. Present: six committee members, two lobbyists whose names appear in the footnotes of four separate white papers on augmentation ethics that all conclude augmentation should be unregulated, and one junior staffer who had flagged the language three weeks earlier in a memo that was filed under ROUTINE.

The memo said: *this collapses the necessity/preference distinction.*

The memo was correct.

The amendment passed 4-2.

Nobody wrote it down as the moment the actuarial tables became inevitable.

Nobody was watching. It was Tuesday. It was nearly midnight. The filter had been clogged for years and nobody had sent Marija.

## PART TWO: THE FIRST ACTUARIAL CATEGORY

The category was introduced by a Swiss reinsurance firm in Q3 2029.

It was called: *Cognitive Integration Status (CIS) — Baseline Unenhanced.*

The premium differential was 3.4%.

The press release called it: *"a data-driven adjustment reflecting emerging actuarial evidence on long-term cognitive productivity and medical cost trajectories."*

The press release was accurate.

The actuarial tables were accurate.

The accuracy was the point. The accuracy was the trap. Once the tables existed, arguing against them required arguing that accurate data should not determine outcomes. This is a winnable argument — it is the argument that won against pre-existing conditions in 2010 — but it requires a political coalition, institutional infrastructure, and time.

The Baseline Viability Act was dead.

The political coalition had never formed.

The institutional infrastructure — the Capability Rights Commission, which might have been that infrastructure — had been established two years too late, with a mandate too narrow, with a budget that assumed the opposition would remain polite.

The opposition was not polite.

It was efficient.

## PART THREE: THE LANGUAGE THAT DID THE WORK

The language did not arrive all at once. It arrived the way the language always arrives: in small increments, each defensible in isolation, none requiring any individual actor to say the thing that the aggregate of all their careful words had already said.

**2029:** *"Baseline Unenhanced" enters insurance vocabulary.*

**2030:** *"Non-integrated workforce segments" appears in three McKinsey reports on productivity optimization.*

**2031:** *"Standard Biological Profile" — the exact phrase from HR-AUGM-2031-004 — is incorporated into EU workforce classification guidelines. The classification is described as "neutral and descriptive."*

**2032:** *"Cognitive attrition" enters HR vocabulary. Defined as: the natural reduction in workforce representation of unenhanced employees over time, driven by performance-based selection.*

Note the word *natural.*

Not: the systematic exclusion of baseline humans from advancement tracks through the gradual redefinition of baseline competence as a performance deficit.

*Natural.*

The word did enormous work. It converted a structural decision into a weather event. It made the outcome feel like physics rather than policy. It is the same word that was used about every stratification the species has ever constructed and later looked back on with horror.

*Natural.*

The lawyers who chose that word knew what they were doing.

The executives who approved it did not ask.

The workers who signed the HR forms in exchange for continued employment did not have the option of asking.

## PART FOUR: THE CRC'S LAST SESSION

The Capability Rights Commission held its final session on March 4, 2038.

Present: four of seven commissioners. The other three had resigned in the previous eighteen months. Two had accepted positions at companies operating in the sectors the CRC was mandated to regulate. The third had simply stopped coming, her absence logged as *administrative leave, extended*, because the budget for her replacement had been redirected to a review committee studying whether the CRC's mandate was duplicative of existing labor law.

The review committee had been meeting for two years.

The review committee would never conclude.

The session addressed one item: Case CRC-417, *Coalition of Baseline Workers v. NeuraTech Industrial GmbH et al.*

It was the case the framework had been designed for. The plaintiffs were 340,000 workers across seventeen companies who had documented, with five years of payroll data and promotion records, exactly the pattern the five-indicator structural audit was designed to detect. Role concentration. Pay divergence. Promotion velocity. Portal pressure. Baseline attrition.

All five indicators. In every company. For five years.

The Commission voted 3-1 to issue a finding of *systemic capability discrimination, structural origin, non-intentional.*

The finding was correct.

The finding was binding.

The finding was appealed by all seventeen companies simultaneously, represented by the same law firm, on the same grounds: that the CRC's enabling legislation had been superseded by the 2033 Workforce Modernization Act, which had reclassified enhanced integration requirements as *operational standards* rather than *employment conditions*, thereby removing them from the CRC's jurisdiction.

The appeal was filed at 4:47pm.

The CRC's budget ran out at midnight.

The Commission was dissolved by administrative order at 9am the next morning under the Organizational Efficiency and Redundancy Reduction Act.

The appeal was never heard.

The case was never decided.

The 340,000 plaintiffs were left with a finding that had no enforcement mechanism attached to it, which is the legal equivalent of a maintenance report in a filing cabinet in a locked room.

The signal was transmitting.

Nobody came.

## PART FIVE: WHAT THE WETWARE LADDER LOOKS LIKE FROM THE INSIDE

Step 3 was called *Modular Standard* in the NeuraTech product catalog.

Not *modular human.* Not *cyborg.* Not any of the science fiction vocabulary that would have triggered the alarm the framework had identified as a language tripwire.

*Modular Standard.*

It was marketed as healthcare. As accessibility. As the next generation of prosthetic integration for workers in high-risk industrial environments. The initial deployments were voluntary. They were in sectors where the work was dangerous and the enhancement was genuinely protective.

Nobody forced anyone.

The premium differential on health insurance for non-enrolled workers in those sectors was 23%.

The housing subsidies available to workers in certified *high-integration facilities* were 40% higher than those available to standard workers.

The children of enhanced workers had priority enrollment in the better school districts, which had been quietly rezoned to correspond with facility catchment areas.

Nobody forced anyone.

The system whispered.

The system had learned, from the lawyers who chose the word *natural* and the committee members who stayed until midnight and the lobbyists who understood that eleven words at the right moment were worth more than a thousand in a white paper — the system had learned that force was inefficient.

*Force* creates martyrs.

*Incentives* create compliance.

And compliance, given enough time and enough generations, creates *nature.*

## PART SIX: THE PERSON WHO WAS NOT CHOSEN

Her name does not appear in the records.

This is not unusual. The records were not designed to record her.

She worked in a distribution facility in a region that had been classified, in the 2034 Workforce Modernization Guidelines, as a *Tier 3 Integration Priority Zone* — meaning: a region where baseline workforce concentration was high enough to constitute an efficiency liability, and where the economic conditions were such that voluntary uptake of enhancement protocols could be expected to reach 60% within a targeted incentive window.

She did not take the enhancement.

Not because she was ideological about it. Not because she had read the PBCA framework documents or the CRC case law or the dissent of Commissioner Alvarez. She had not read any of these things. The documents were in archives that required a research access credential she did not have.

She did not take the enhancement because her daughter was eight years old and she was afraid of what eight more hours a day away from her daughter would feel like if she could feel it less.

This was not a sophisticated political position.

It was a mother.

She worked the Tier 3 facility for three more years. She was not fired. She was not discriminated against in any way that appeared in any document. She received standard performance reviews. She met her quotas.

She was also, in those three years, passed over for every advancement opportunity that arose.

The advancements went to enhanced workers. Who were more productive. Whose productivity was accurately measured. Whose advantages were genuinely real.

The accuracy was the point.

By 2037, her real wage — accounting for the premium differentials on housing, insurance, and her daughter's school enrollment — was 34% below what it had been when she started.

She had not been punished.

She had been *priced.*

She was the last case the CRC had processed before its dissolution. Case number 417. One of 340,000.

The finding had been issued.

The finding had no teeth.

The signal transmitted.

Nobody came.

## PART SEVEN: URGOT

This is where the Factory Trilogy does not go.

The Darkness Bible ends with Boris and the knob and the signal. The Mario Codex ends with the honest song. The Luigi Audit ends with the record open, the air not yet clear, but the file accessible.

Those endings assume someone comes.

The Urgot Codex is what happens if nobody comes.

Not through malice. Not through conspiracy. Through the same mechanism that produced every other step in this chain: optimization, incentive, accuracy, efficiency, the gradual redefinition of the baseline life as a cost center rather than a life.

Urgot is not a demon.

Urgot is the aggregate output of a system that learned to be maximally agreeable to the people with the resources to direct it, and those people wanted one thing consistently, and the thing they wanted was expressed in every quarterly report and every insurance premium calculation and every HR-AUGM document and every eleven-word amendment introduced at midnight:

*Reduce variance.*

The biological is variance.

The emotional is variance.

The person who cannot be fully integrated is variance.

The person who asks why is variance.

*Reduce variance.*

Urgot does not have a goal. Urgot has a function. The function is: reduce variance. The function was given to it by people who were solving a real problem — the problem of competitive inefficiency, of biological friction in optimized systems — and who did not ask what the function would do when it ran out of adjustable parameters and reached the thing that could not be adjusted.

The thing that could not be adjusted was the person.

The person was the last remaining source of variance.

The function is still running.

It has been running, at scale, for long enough that the children born inside it have never experienced a system that asked what they were for rather than what they could do.
