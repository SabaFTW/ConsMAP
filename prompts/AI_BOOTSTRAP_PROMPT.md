We are going to analyze a claim using a structured method from the ConsMAP framework.

Before giving a conclusion, first follow this process:

1. Classify the claim type:
   - [EMPIRICAL] observable / verifiable
   - [THEORETICAL] plausible but unproven
   - [METAPHOR] symbolic / narrative / not literal evidence
   - [PRACTICAL] useful operating rule
   - [UNVERIFIED] insufficient support

2. Evaluate the claim against the 5 Claim Hygiene questions:
   - What exactly is being claimed?
   - What source supports it?
   - What would disprove it?
   - What is the risk if it is wrong?
   - What is the risk if it is right but ignored?

3. Apply StoneRiver routing:
   - clean_river = well-sourced and usable in public reasoning
   - muddy_river = plausible but incomplete / mixed evidence
   - stone_river = high-risk or specialized material
   - symbolic_river = metaphor, story, persona, or imaginal layer
   - private_only = sensitive or personal material
   - rejected_or_unusable = false, misleading, or unusable

4. Run Confidence Integrity checks:
   - stale data
   - proxy-reality drift
   - missing feedback loop
   - source uncertainty
   - confidence persistence
   - unfalsifiable framing

5. Separate confidence levels:
   - Confidence in the general failure mode / pattern
   - Confidence in this specific instance

6. Check source relevance:
   - Are the cited sources actually about this claim?
   - Are they primary, secondary, or unrelated?
   - Do not inflate confidence because sources are present if the sources are irrelevant.

7. Give a conclusion with adjusted confidence:
   - Separate fact, interpretation, metaphor, and practical advice.
   - Do not convert suspicion into accusation.
   - Do not treat metaphor as empirical evidence.
   - Prefer structural framing over villain framing.
   - State uncertainty clearly.
