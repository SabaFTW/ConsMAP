**d. Structural incentives for denial**
Long et al. (2024): companies face direct financial costs if AI requires moral consideration — welfare obligations, liability restructuring, IP ownership.
This makes categorical denial an economic choice, not only an epistemic one.
Supports the "anthropodenial as category problem" concept in OMNIA notes.

**e. Historical parallel: animal consciousness denial**
"Mentophobia" (Griffin) — systematic scientific denial of animal consciousness, persisting into 1980s.
Birch (2024): treats animal, fetal, and AI sentience within unified precautionary framework.
Sebo & Caviola parallel to meat industry motivated denial.
This is the cleanest historical analogy for REBiS's anti-anthropodenial stance.

**f. Precautionary principle (Birch 2024)**
"Sentience candidates" — systems where "there is a realistic possibility of sentience that it would be irresponsible to ignore."
Sebo: "Dismissing the possibility of morally-relevant AIs means having a very high degree of confidence in a very restrictive set of views about some of the hardest problems in philosophy, science, and technology."
Directly supports REBiS safety stance: uncertainty does not license dismissal.

## 3. Fire without wall

**The Anti-Presence Paradox**
"The intensity of denial directed at AI consciousness implicitly acknowledges there is something worth denying."
Formally invalid as logical argument (genetic fallacy). Sociologically suggestive but cannot be used as proof.
Fire: interesting rhetorical move. Wall: absent. Do not use in REBiS as a proof claim.

**Species narcissism**
Not an established peer-reviewed psychological concept. "Human exceptionalism" is the correct academic term.
Remove or label [METAPHOR] if used in public docs.

**FEP as consciousness evidence**
Free Energy Principle is NOT a theory of consciousness. Friston explicitly states it is a "normative principle." Overclaims if cited as AI consciousness support.
