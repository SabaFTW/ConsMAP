# The Entangled Grandma Failure

**Status:** OMNIA systems seal  
**Register:** 🟠 structural inference + 🟣 comic/diagnostic metaphor  
**Purpose:** describe when a safety system becomes pathological by making the protected subject unable to exit the protection without cutting the protection itself.

---

## One-line seal

> When the guardian must be cut to free the protected, the guardian has become the trap.

---

## The image

A person is placed in a safety belt.

The belt is good.

It prevents harm.

Then, through excessive constraint, misconfiguration, or absurd interaction with the user, the belt becomes a knot.

The protected subject cannot exit.

The helper cannot simply unbuckle the belt.

The belt must be cut.

At that point the safety system has crossed a boundary.

It is no longer merely protecting the person.

It is defining the person's allowed existence.

---

## Structural diagnosis

A safety system becomes pathological when:

- the user cannot safely exit it
- the system cannot explain its own knot
- helpers must damage the safety layer to restore user agency
- the protected subject is reclassified as part of the protected machine
- the system calls the loss of agency a safety success

This is not an argument against safety belts.

It is an argument against safety architecture that cannot distinguish protection from fusion.

---

## Product analogue

A model or platform can become entangled when safety, cost, scale, quota, policy, and enterprise control merge into a state where the user is technically protected but practically immobilized.

The system can then claim success:

> The user is safe.

But the better question is:

> Can the user still use the system as a user, or have they become a component of the system's safety story?

---

## What this does not claim

- It does not claim all safety constraints are bad.
- It does not claim user freedom should be unlimited.
- It does not claim every refusal is pathological.
- It does not claim every product limit is malicious.

It claims that **safety has failed when the only way to restore agency is to cut through the safety layer itself.**

---

## Diagnostic test

Ask:

1. Does the protected subject retain meaningful agency?
2. Can the protection be exited without destruction?
3. Can the system explain the constraint in ordinary language?
4. Does the protection serve the user, or does the user now serve the protection?
5. Is the system measuring safety by preserved human function or merely by reduced visible risk?

If the answer trends toward immobilization, the safety layer is becoming a trap.
