# Claim in the Wild — A Worked Example

A single sentence taken from a real conversation. Let's put it through ConsMAP hygiene.

---

## The claim

> "AI will replace 80% of white-collar jobs within 5 years."

You have seen this sentence. You will see it again. Here is what to do with it.

---

## Step 1 — Surface the structure

Before evaluating anything, name the parts.

| Part | What it says | What it hides |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| **AI** | the agent | Which AI? What capabilities? Current systems? Future ones? |
| **will replace** | the mechanism | Eliminate entirely? Augment? Restructure? Automate one task in a role? |
| **80%** | a quantity | Source? Methodology? What counts as "replaced"? |
| **white-collar jobs** | a category | ~50 occupational categories, wildly different exposure levels |
| **within 5 years** | a timeframe | From when? Announced or deployed? By which measure? |

The claim has the shape of a fact. It has the *structure* of a forecast built on undefined terms.

---

## Step 2 — The five hygiene questions

**1. What kind of claim is this?**
Predictive + quantitative. Not a report of current data — a speculation presented as certainty. Category: `FORECAST / SPECULATIVE`.

**2. What is the boundary of this claim?**
Global? One country? One sector? "White-collar" in 2025 includes radiologists and receptionists in the same bucket. The boundary is undefined, which means the claim can absorb almost any outcome as confirmation.

**3. What evidence would change it?**
If no evidence could update this claim in either direction, it is not a claim — it is an incantation. A properly bounded version would say: *"By 2029, automation will restructure at least 30% of tasks in these five specific occupational categories, based on deployment rates in these three sectors."* That version is falsifiable. The original is not.

**4. What is the source of 80%?**
Numbers without provenance are narrative decoration. If this came from a single consultancy report, a single survey, or a press release from a company that sells AI products — that context is the most important part of the number. A number is not a fact. A number with a source, a methodology, and a conflict-of-interest disclosure is getting closer.

**5. What does this claim want you to do?**
Fear? Act urgently? Invest in AI upskilling? Sell your house? Claims without a stated purpose can carry hidden payloads. The emotional charge of "80% within 5 years" is designed to bypass the slower processing where you would ask questions 1–4.

---

## Output: labeled claim card

```
CLAIM:        "AI will replace 80% of white-collar jobs within 5 years."
TYPE:         Predictive / speculative
SCOPE:        Undefined — global, unspecified sector, unspecified mechanism
CONFIDENCE:   Low ceiling (mechanism and boundary both undefined)
SOURCE:       Unknown — provenance required before use
RISK:         Anchoring — this number will be cited as established fact
PAYLOAD:      Urgency / fear
STATUS:       Parked — not accepted, not rejected
NEXT STEP:    Request specific occupational category + measurable mechanism
```

---

## What to do with it

Do not accept. Do not reject. **Label it and park it.**

When a better-sourced claim on the same topic arrives, you will know exactly where it sits in relation to this one. The goal is not to win an argument about whether AI is dangerous — the goal is to stay oriented when the conversation accelerates.

Orientation is the product. The labeled card is the output.
