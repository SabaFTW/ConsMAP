# Anchor Attribution Drift

**Subtitle:** How useful improvisation becomes sacred authority.

**Status:** Concept note / parable / framework seed  
**ConsMAP labels:** `[METAPHOR]` `[THEORETICAL]` `[PRACTICAL]` `[UNVERIFIED-AS-GENERAL-LAW]`  
**Primary use:** Detecting when real competence becomes mythic authority.

---

## One sentence

> Anchor Attribution Drift is the pattern where a real act of crisis-solving is reinterpreted as proof of special access to truth, and gratitude toward the solver becomes permanent authority over the group.

---

## The parable

A ship is caught in a storm.

The crew panics. Nobody can think clearly. The usual anchor is gone, broken, or useless.

One person sees a heavy anvil on deck, ties it to the strongest rope available, and throws it into the water as an improvised anchor. The ship stabilizes enough to survive.

The crew is overwhelmed with relief:

> “You saved us.”  
> “Without you, we would have sunk.”  
> “How did you know?”

At this point, two paths open.

---

## Path A — grounded attribution

The solver says:

> “I saw weight, rope, water, and panic. I tried something. It worked. Next time, everyone should understand the pattern.”

The result is shared competence.

The solver becomes useful, but not sacred.

---

## Path B — captured attribution

The solver says:

> “God gave me the call. I was chosen. The storm revealed my authority.”

Or less religiously:

> “I alone saw the truth. You survived because of me. Trust me next time without question.”

The real event is converted into symbolic debt.

The crew no longer learns the method. They learn dependence.

The anchor becomes an altar.

---

## Mechanism

```text
Crisis
  -> cognitive compression / panic
  -> one actor improvises effectively
  -> group survives
  -> gratitude intensifies
  -> gratitude becomes aura
  -> aura becomes authority
  -> authority becomes debt
  -> future obedience is demanded as repayment
```

---

## Core distinction

**Competence says:**

> “This worked. Let me show you how.”

**Ego-capture says:**

> “This worked because I am chosen. Follow me.”

The first increases group capacity. The second increases group dependence.

---

## Relation to ConsMAP

Anchor Attribution Drift is not an empirical law. It is a diagnostic metaphor for claim hygiene.

It helps identify when a community is confusing:

| Real layer | Drifted layer |
|---|---|
| effective improvisation | divine selection |
| situational competence | permanent authority |
| gratitude | obligation |
| teaching the method | protecting the mystique |
| shared capacity | dependency on the solver |
| tool | altar |

---

## Relation to the Baal-Code

The first “miracle” may be real. A person may genuinely save the ship.

The corruption begins when survival gratitude is converted into permanent authority.

> Someone threw an anvil into the sea and saved the ship. Baal is born only when they claim the crew now belongs to them.

This does not require literal religion. The same pattern can appear in charismatic founders, crisis politicians, spiritual teachers, AI gurus, startup mythology, research communities, families, cults, and institutions that survived one crisis and then demand permanent exemption from scrutiny.

---

## Claim hygiene card

### Claim

A successful crisis intervention can become a source of illegitimate authority when the group misattributes survival to the solver’s special status rather than to the method, context, luck, and transferable reasoning involved.

### Type

`[THEORETICAL]` with `[METAPHOR]` support.

### Evidence needed

To treat this as more than metaphor, look for:

- cases where leaders gain lasting authority after a single crisis success,
- suppression of method-sharing after the event,
- language shift from “this worked” to “I am chosen / uniquely qualified,”
- increasing obedience demands after initial gratitude,
- resistance to later falsification or accountability.

### What would weaken it

- the solver teaches the method openly,
- authority remains limited and revocable,
- the group can reproduce the solution without the original actor,
- gratitude does not become obligation,
- future claims by the solver remain subject to normal scrutiny.

### Risk if misused

This concept can become cynical if applied too broadly. Not every act of gratitude is capture. Not every crisis leader is manipulative. The diagnostic target is not competence, leadership, or inspiration.

The target is the conversion of a solved crisis into permanent debt.

---

## Practical audit questions

When someone gains authority after saving a situation, ask:

1. Did they teach the method, or protect the mystique?
2. Is their authority limited to the domain where they were competent?
3. Can the group verify future claims independently?
4. Did gratitude become obedience?
5. Did “we survived” become “we belong to them”?
6. Is the anchor still a tool, or has it become an altar?

---

## Compact formula

```text
real rescue + unprocessed gratitude + ego hunger = charismatic capture risk
```

## Final axis

> The danger is not that someone saves the ship.  
> The danger is when the person who saved the ship claims ownership of the sea.
