## Why the Factory Trilogy Belongs in ConsMAP

ConsMAP already separates symbolic reading from literalization. This corpus needs that discipline.

The Factory Trilogy is valuable because it makes a difficult pattern visible:

1. **The mechanism was real.**  
   The camera really needed less glare. The filter really needed cleaning. The hydraulic seal really failed.

2. **The meaning was also real.**  
   Workers needed morale, songs, shared memory, and continuity.

3. **The capture happened at the interface.**  
   A knob stopped being a knob and became a permission structure. A schedule stopped being maintenance and became liturgy. A file stopped being a file and became outranked by myth.

4. **The audit did not destroy meaning.**  
   Mario and Luigi do not abolish song. They remove fraud from song.

5. **The final answer is maintenance.**  
   The system is not saved by final revelation. The filter is clean until it is not.

This is why Factory is not a side-joke. It is the maintenance department of the whole framework.

## Guardrails

### Do not literalize the corpus.

Bad reading:

> The Factory Trilogy proves hidden spiritual machinery.

Clean reading:

> The Factory Trilogy is a fictional systems parable for how institutions misclassify mechanisms as meaning and then capture the interface.

### Do not amputate the symbolic layer.

Bad reading:

> This is just mythology and therefore useless.

Clean reading:

> The symbolic layer compresses system dynamics. Extract the structure, then ground it.

### Do not collapse evidence levels.

A research dossier on Umwelt or hardware constraints is not the same type of object as a fictional gospel about a maintenance mouse.

Both can belong in the same map only if their registers remain distinct.

### Do not let the audit become a throne.

The pattern-recognizer can become the next institution.
The third tribe that says "we see the pattern" can become upgraded packaging for the same loop.

ConsMAP must keep the audit discipline pointed back toward claim hygiene and maintenance.
