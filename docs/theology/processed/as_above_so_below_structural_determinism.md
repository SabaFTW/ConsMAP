1. Jakob von Uexküll’s Umwelt Theory
Established facts
Definition. Uexküll (1864–1944), a Baltic-German biologist, coined Umwelt (literally
“surround-world”) to designate the species-specific perceptual world constituted by an
organism’s sensors (Merkorgan) and effectors (Werkorgan) operating in a feedback
loop. Crucially, Umwelt is distinguished from Umgebung — the “objective” surroundings
as observed by an external (typically human) observer (Wikipedia: Umwelt; Wikipedia:
Jakob Johann von Uexküll).
Canonical example — the tick. Uexküll showed that the tick’s entire perceptual
universe consists of three signs: butyric acid (mammal sweat), 37°C (mammal warmth),
and the tactile sense of skin. Light, color, sound, the rest of the forest — none of it exists
for the tick. Giorgio Agamben’s reading: “this eyeless animal finds the way to her
watchpoint…with the help of only its skin’s general sensitivity to light”

Wikipedia

(Wikipedia: Umwelt).
Other canonical examples. The black ghost knifefish (electrical fields), the
echolocating bat (air-compression waves),

Edge.org

the sea urchin, jellyfish, and

amoeba — each builds a distinct phenomenal world from what its sensorium admits
(Edge.org: The Umwelt, David Eagleman).
Mechanism. “The small subset of the world that an animal is able to detect is its
umwelt. The bigger reality, whatever that might mean, is called the umgebung … each
organism presumably assumes its umwelt to be the entire objective reality ‘out there’”
(Edge.org).
Influence. Direct intellectual lineage to Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, Cassirer, Deleuze &
Guattari, Maturana, Sebeok’s biosemiotics, and contemporary enactivism/4E cognition

(Wikipedia: Jakob Johann von Uexküll; The Philosopher: Feiten on Umwelt).

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: That sensory apparatus filters available stimuli is uncontroversial empirical
biology, vindicated by neuroethology (e.g., Ed Yong’s An Immense World, 2022,
Wikipedia

builds the popular case from extensive contemporary research) (Wikipedia:

Umwelt).
Contested: Uexküll’s constructivist gloss — that organisms are “subjects” actively
constituting meaning — leans Kantian and is criticized as either unfalsifiable or
anthropomorphic. Tim Elmo Feiten frames the central tension: Umwelt is simultaneously
(a) an empirical model of sensory selection and (b) a speculative metaphysics of
subjective experience. The two projects do not always cohere (The Philosopher).
Important historical caveat (use sparingly, but it is forensically relevant if you cite
him as authority): Uexküll was anti-Darwinian, signed the 1933 Confession of German
Professors to Adolf Hitler, and in 1934 co-founded (with Heidegger, Schmitt,
Rosenberg) the Committee for Philosophy of Law of the Academy for German Law. His
Staatsbiologie extended Umwelt into an organicist totalitarian political theory
The Philosopher

(Wikipedia: Jakob Johann von Uexküll; Tandfonline: Arguing With

Uexküll’s Umwelten). For a “cold analytical” essay, this is a feature, not a bug — it shows
what happens when species-specific phenomenology is reified into normative politics.

Useful for the essay
Lion / grass example (your framing is supportable). A lion’s musculoskeletal system
is built for ambush, sprint, neck-bite kills; obligate carnivore digestion makes cellulose
useless; therefore grass has no signal value. Grass is not “ignored” — in the strict
Uexküllian sense it does not enter the lion’s Umwelt as a meaningful object. Frame this
as: structure → permitted actions → category of “real.” This is the strongest plank of
structural determinism.
Counterexample to flag for honesty: Behavioral plasticity. Falconry research (Lestel,
Bussolini, Chrulew) shows that humans and raptors can co-construct a hybrid Umwelt
across species lines — limiting strict structural determinism (Tandfonline: Concept of
Umwelt and Anthropology Beyond the Human).

Recommended modern bridge for “AI Umwelt”
The Sebeok/Ziemke/Sharkey extension of Umwelt to “robots and artificial life” (Semiotica
134, 2001) and recent papers on triadic Umwelten incorporating technics make the bridge
to Section 3 (silicon) academically defensible rather than merely metaphorical

(ResearchGate: Uexküll concept and anthropology).

2. Vertebrate Bone Evolution: Calcium Carbonate → Calcium
Phosphate
Established facts
The switch. Vertebrates are nearly unique among Metazoa in possessing skeletons of
calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite, ~Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂) rather than the calcium
carbonate (CaCO₃) that dominates invertebrate exoskeletons. The transition occurred
over the ~480-million-year history of vertebrate bone,

Semantic Scholar

with first bony

vertebrate fossils (jawless ostracoderms — Astraspis, Arandaspis) appearing in the
Middle Ordovician (~470–480 Ma)

Natural History Museum

(Acta Orthopaedica: Where

did bone come from?; PMC: Where did bone come from?; EBSCO: Earliest vertebrate
fossils).
The metabolic / acidosis hypothesis (Ruben & Bennett, 1980, 1981, 1987). This is
the dominant working explanation. Vertebrates engage in intense bursts of activity
supported primarily by rapid intramuscular formation of lactic acid, followed by
protracted systemic acidosis. Calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) is far more pH-stable
than calcium carbonate; CaCO₃ would dissolve under repeated lactic-acid loads,
“exacerbating” postactive skeletal dissolution and hypercalcemia (PubMed: The
Evolution of Bone, Ruben 1987; Wiley: The vertebrate bone hypothesis, 2024).
The phosphorus-storage hypothesis. Bone functions as a phosphate reservoir for
ATP/energy metabolism — “all cellular processes (and thus, life) are ultimately
dependent on phosphate given its central role in energy metabolism” (PMC: Skeletal
endocrinology).
Direct quote (Ruben & Bennett): “We suggest the calcium phosphate skeleton is
distinctly advantageous to vertebrates because of their relatively unusual and ancient
pattern of activity metabolism: intense bursts of activity supported primarily by rapid
intramuscular formation of lactic acid” (Oxford Academic: Evolution of Bone).
The mechanical formula isn’t pure. Bone hydroxyapatite incorporates substituting
ions: (Ca,Na,Mg,K,Sr,Pb)₁₀ (PO₄,CO₃,SO₄)₆ (OH,F,Cl,CO₃)₂ — composition is partially
environment-dependent (Wiley 2024).

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: The chemistry — CaPO₄ is materially less soluble at low pH than CaCO₃ — is

laboratory-verifiable; the in-vivo correlation with anaerobic activity is well-documented
across living vertebrates (American Physiological Society, Physiology 2014).
Thinner: Causal direction. Did anaerobic burst metabolism select for CaPO₄, or did
CaPO₄ availability enable burst metabolism? The fossil record cannot fully disambiguate.
Multiple competing hypotheses (phosphate sequestration as energy stores,

Actaorthop

exoskeletal armor against eurypterid predators, electroreceptor function in dermal
denticles) coexist in the literature (Acta Orthopaedica).

The chain to behavior — the load-bearing argument for your essay
1. CaPO₄ permits sustained lactic acid metabolism without skeletal dissolution.
2. Lactic acid metabolism enables explosive muscular output (chasing, lifting, fighting).
3. Skeletal geometry + muscular output sets the action repertoire (the “permitted actions”
that enter the Umwelt as relevant).
4. The action repertoire defines what the organism can interact with — and therefore what
is real for it in the Uexküllian sense.
This is the chain through which mineralogy → ontology.

Useful for the essay — elephant case
Elephants are the textbook case of skeletal structure as a behavioral constraint rather
than affordance. Their columnar limbs (analogous to sauropods) impose what
biomechanists describe as a “negative limb compliance gradient … suggesting an
important locomotory constraint preventing achievement of a full-body aerial phase
during locomotion”

arxiv

(arXiv: Salient features in locomotor evolutionary adaptations

of proboscideans; PMC: Three-dimensional locomotor dynamics of elephants).
Even at maximum speed, elephants never have all four feet off the ground
simultaneously — they cannot gallop. Bone weight–length ratio is ~205 g/cm, far higher
than horses or cattle

Academia.edu

(Academia.edu: Kinematics of elephant limb bones).

Forensic application: the elephant’s skeletal mass literally subtracts certain behaviors
(galloping, jumping, climbing) from its possibility space; therefore certain phenomenal
categories (mid-air, the experience of pursuit at >25 km/h, vertical predation) are not in
its Umwelt.

Established facts
TPUs are radically constrained, not general. Norman Jouppi’s 2017 ISCA paper
documents that the TPU’s heart is a 65,536 8-bit MAC matrix multiply unit (peak 92
TOPS), with deterministic execution and no branch prediction, out-of-order execution,
multithreading, or speculative caches: “The lack of such features helps explain why …
the TPU is relatively small and low power”

arxiv

(arXiv: In-Datacenter Performance

Analysis of a TPU).
Google’s 8th-generation TPU (TPU 8t/8i, 2025–2026 rollout) splits training and
inference into fundamentally different chips because the workloads have diverged —
agentic AI loops magnify small inefficiencies. The split is itself an admission that one
architecture cannot host all forms of machine cognition (Google Blog: 8th-generation
TPU; Knowledge Hub Media).
Memory bandwidth as ontological horizon. “Memory bandwidth bottlenecks: CPUs
typically provide 50–100 GB/s memory bandwidth, while GPUs offer 1–3 TB/s. For neural
networks, data movement often dominates execution time… The Von Neumann
bottleneck, where computation and memory share a bus, fundamentally constrains
performance” (Thepurplestruct: CPU vs GPU vs TPU).

only with TensorFlow and JAX; PyTorch support is suboptimal.

The exact analogy to Umwelt
The TPU’s systolic array is to matrix multiplication what the lion’s musculature is to
grass: the substrate physically cannot extract relevance from inputs that don’t
decompose into its native operation. Neurosymbolic workloads on a TPU exhibit ~75×
speedup deficit on systolic-array hardware versus purpose-built reconfigurable chips —
the “ontological horizon” of the TPU literally excludes symbolic reasoning at scale until
the substrate changes (arXiv: CogSys).

Contested speculation (flag in essay): That this constitutes a strict ontological
horizon analogous to biology. The disanalogy: silicon is redesignable on ~3-year cycles;
bone took ~480 Myr. Substrate plasticity is far higher in the technical case — which is
exactly Section 7’s point about antifragility.

4. Gregory Bateson’s Double Bind Theory
Established facts
Origin. Coined in Bateson, Jackson, Haley & Weakland, “Toward a Theory of
Schizophrenia,” Behavioral Science 1(4): 251–254 (1956),

Wiley Online Library

based on

work at the Veterans Administration Hospital, Palo Alto (Wiley: Toward a theory of
schizophrenia; Solutions-Centre PDF).
Structural conditions (the necessary ingredients per the original paper):
1. Two or more persons in an emotionally important ongoing relationship.
2. A primary negative injunction (“Do X or be punished”).
3. A secondary negative injunction at a different logical level that contradicts the first
(“Don’t see this as a punishment-context”).

4. A tertiary injunction prohibiting escape from the field.
5. Repeated experience until the structure becomes internalized (Solutions-Centre;
Wikipedia: Double bind).
Theoretical basis. Russell & Whitehead’s Principia Mathematica Theory of Logical
Types: a class cannot be a member of itself; signals at one level cannot directly resolve
contradictions at another. The schizophrenic, on this view, fails to comprehend the
structure of the communicating situation and “cannot respond in a purposeful way”
(Barnard: Schizophrenic Techniques).
Bateson’s porpoise example (1969 NIMH symposium): a porpoise trained on noveltyreward, then deprived of food unless it produces a new trick — starved if it repeats,
starved if it doesn’t — was driven into pathological behavior. A textbook clean
experimental analog (Barnard).

Strong vs. thin evidence
Thin / contested: The empirical claim that double binds cause schizophrenia has not
survived. Twin and adoption studies established a strong genetic component; familyinteraction theories are now considered, at best, contributory or not falsifiable in the
original form (Goertzel: Double Bind Theory Revisited, Koopmans).
Strong: As a structural description of pathological systems — communicative situations
where contradictory demands at different logical levels with no exit produce dysfunction
— the model is generative and has been productively applied beyond psychiatry to
organizations (Systems Thinking Alliance: Double Bind) and to natural-selection
paradoxes (Bateson used Carroll’s “Bread and Butter Fly,” doomed if it finds food and
doomed if it doesn’t, to model evolutionary double binds — Wikipedia: Double bind).

Direct application to “identity feedback loops”
Bateson explicitly extended the double-bind frame across logical levels — between
individual and family, between genotype and phenotype: “the problems of adaptation
and learning and their pathologies must be considered in terms of a hierarchic system in
which stochastic change occurs at the boundary points between the segments of the
hierarchy” (Wikipedia: Double bind).
Application: when a stable identity (high-level self-model) issues demands on its own
substrate (the body, the organization, the silicon, the user base) that the substrate
cannot satisfy without violating other constraints it requires for stability, the system
enters a Bateson-style double bind. The pathology is not optional — it is structural.
René Girard explicitly absorbed Bateson, arguing the double bind is more universal than

schizogenic family interaction: the imitative model says “imitate me / do not imitate me”
— the founding command of mimetic rivalry (Boreas: The Double Bind and
Schizophrenia). This is useful for any argument about identity-as-pointer (Section 8).

Bateson on contemporary information societies
“Bateson went so far as to suggest that schizophrenia is one of the standard states we all
live in within contemporary information societies” — pathology becomes ambient when
contradictory communicative structures are inescapable (Barnard). Use sparingly; this is a
quotable framing, not a vindicated empirical claim.

5. Adrian Bejan’s Constructal Law
Established facts
The law (1996). “For a finite-size flow system to persist in time (to live), it must evolve
in such a way that it provides easier access to the imposed (global) currents that flow
through it” (Wikipedia: Adrian Bejan; Duke MEMS; PMC: Constructal law of design and
evolution).
Empirical predictions. From the constructal law, Bejan derives:
Metabolic rate ∝ M^(3/4)
Frequency (breathing, heart) ∝ M^(−1/4)
Hair diameter ∝ body length^(1/2)
Respiratory surface ∝ M^(7/8)
Speed ∝ M^(1/6) and stride/flap frequency ∝ M^(−1/6) — the same scaling rules
govern running, swimming, and flying (PMC; Bejan & Marden, J Exp Biol 2006).
Convergent evolution claim. The dolphin/fish/penguin/Boeing-747 result: all forms of
locomotion balance vertical work-loss (impact/lift) against horizontal friction-loss; “the
same proportionalities rule optimal running, flying and swimming … not a coincidence; it
is an illustration of the fact that a universal principle is involved” (Bejan) (Penn State:
Unified Physics Theory; Duke Today 2009).
Marden’s complement to Gould. Steven Jay Gould argued evolution is contingent —
rerun the tape, get different animals. Marden, on the constructal law: “even though you
couldn’t predict exactly what animals would look like if you started evolution over … with
a given gravity and density of their tissues, the same basic patterns of their design
would evolve again” (Penn State).

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: The empirical predictions about scaling and locomotion are testable and
broadly fit data across orders of magnitude in body mass.
Contested — and seriously so. A critical literature exists. Liqiu Wang’s Critique of
Constructal Theory (Cambridge Scholars, 2019) examines applications “in street design,
economics, heat transfer optimization, flow systems, and explanations of natural
structures and social phenomena” and shows “the theory has significant flaws and is
even not applicable in certain circumstances” (Cambridge Scholars: Critique of
Constructal Theory). A separate Medium critique notes the constructal law remains
“outside the mainstream discourse in contemporary theoretical physics, astrophysics,
and cosmology” despite Bejan’s prolific output (Medium: A Critique of Constructal
Theory).
Latent teleology problem. The phrasing “evolves in order to facilitate flow” reads as
teleological. Bejan defends this as a description of an emergent equilibrium, not a goal
— but critics note this is a familiar move in pseudoscientific physics-mimicry. Forensic
note for the essay: if you cite Bejan, distinguish carefully between the strong empirical
claim (similar selection pressures yield similar designs) and the strong universal claim (a
single law of physics underlies all design). The first is solid; the second is contested.

How to use it in the essay
Bejan supplies the anti-mysticism move: “as above, so below” similarities between
biology and technology need not invoke any Hermetic correspondence. Convergent
design is what efficiency pressure does in any flow system — biological, computational,
organizational — given comparable constraints. This is exactly the cold framing the
essay’s title implies.
Counterexamples to acknowledge: Many evolutionary outcomes are not efficient
(vestigial structures, recurrent laryngeal nerve, the inverted vertebrate retina). These are
tolerated by selection because they don’t impair flow enough to be culled. The
constructal law overpredicts; the actual record is messier.

6. Institutional Capture Sequence
Knights Templar (1119–1314) — decentralized financial network → crown
capture
Founded 1119 to protect pilgrims; Pope-protected, exempt from royal taxation. Built a
cross-continental network of preceptories from London to Jerusalem (TheCollector:

Knights Templar & Modern Banking; BankFrogs).
Innovation: encrypted letters of credit (using a Maltese-cross cipher). Pilgrim
deposits gold in London, draws funds in Jerusalem; physical specie never moves. “The
first international wire transfer, the original traveller’s cheque — seven centuries before
American Express” (Histories and Castles).
Captured. By the late 13th century, the French treasury was housed at the Paris Temple;
Philip IV was deeply in debt to the Order. On Friday 13 October 1307, Philip ordered
simultaneous arrests of French Templars; under torture they confessed to heresy. Pope
Clement V’s bull Pastoralis praeeminentiae (1307) ordered Christian monarchs to arrest
Templars; Vox in excelso and Ad providam (1312) dissolved the order and transferred
assets nominally to the Hospitallers — but Philip extracted “large sums” for the French
crown during the transfer and held French Templar lands. Jacques de Molay was burned
at the stake on 18 March 1314 (TheCollector; Foreign Exchanges: Today in European
History; Periscope Global: Knights Templars and the Invention of Modern Banking).
Mechanism: A privately-controlled, supranational financial network became
indispensable to sovereigns; sovereigns then captured (or destroyed) it when the
dependency reversed direction. The Chinon Parchment (rediscovered in Vatican
archives) showed Clement had secretly cleared the Templars of heresy — politically too
late (BankFrogs).

Council of Nicaea (325 CE) — distributed Christian networks → state
doctrine
Pre-Constantinian Christianity was a “franchise model” — geographically distributed,
doctrinally heterogeneous (Arian, Nicene/homoousian, Modalist, Adoptionist, etc.), with
no centralized creed (Predictive History: Council of Nicaea).
Constantine’s intervention. After defeating Licinius (324) to become sole emperor,
Constantine convened ~200–318 bishops at Nicaea (May–July 325), funded their travel,
presided over the opening session in Latin (most attendees spoke Greek), and afterward
used civil power to enforce the verdict — exiling Arius. “This was the beginning of the
practice of using secular power to establish doctrinal orthodoxy within Christianity”
(Wikipedia: First Council of Nicaea; Christian History Institute).
Quote (Christian History Institute): “If Christianity were to serve as the cement of the
Empire, however, it had to hold one faith. So the emperors called for church councils like
Nicea, paid the way for bishops to attend, and pressed church leaders for doctrinal
unity. The age of Christian emperors was an age of creeds; and creeds were the
instruments of conformity” (Christian History Institute).
Recent revisionist scholarship. Rev. Dr. Kathleen Griffin (2025 Nicaea anniversary

conference, World Council of Churches): Constantine “employed Christianity as a tool
for political unity, transforming it into an ‘imperial religion’ to strengthen his hold on
power … merging ‘creed, sword, and empire.’” Note that he retained the title pontifex
maximus and was not baptized until his deathbed (World Council of Churches).
Forensic note. A common false claim — that the biblical canon was decided at Nicaea —
originates with Voltaire and has no basis. Don’t repeat it (Wikipedia: First Council of
Nicaea).

Internet → platform monopolies
TCP/IP and HTTP were architected for end-to-end interoperability; permissionless
innovation was the design ethos. Platforms (Amazon, Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft)
progressively limited interoperability to entrench dominance: “low levels of
interoperability exclude smaller platforms from sharing in the value of the large network,
and as a result, they face many barriers to competition” (Substack: Interoperability and
Platform Monopoly).
Network economics (the standard explanation): low marginal cost of additional users +
demand-side network effects + data advantages → “winner-takes-most” dynamics.
Joseph Farrell & Michael Katz: “In network markets subject to technological progress,
competition may take the form of a succession of ‘temporary monopolists’ who displace
one another through innovation. Such competition is often called Schumpeterian rivalry”
(ITIF: Monopoly Myths).
The platforms now constitute infrastructure: “you can’t do much online without your
data being routed through one of these giants” (Economic Security Project).

The structural mechanism — the Iron Law of Oligarchy
This is the keystone for a “thermodynamic inevitability” argument.
Robert Michels, Political Parties (1911): “It is organization which gives dominion of the
elected over the electors. … Who says organization, says oligarchy.” Any organization
above a certain size requires division of labor, specialized expertise, and bureaucratic
hierarchy; these requirements predictably concentrate power in a managerial elite,
regardless of the founding ideology (Wikipedia: Iron law of oligarchy; Britannica;
Encyclopedia.com).
Darcy K. Leach’s compression: “Bureaucracy happens. If bureaucracy happens, power
rises. Power corrupts” (Wikipedia: Iron law of oligarchy).
Modern empirical confirmation. Heaberlin & DeDeo (2016) data-mined a decade of
Wikipedia governance and found oligarchic concentration consistent with Michels —

even on a platform explicitly designed for distributed editing. Similar findings exist for
Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs) (Wikipedia: Iron law of oligarchy).

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: That centralization tends to occur. Historical record is overdetermined.
Contested: That it’s “thermodynamically inevitable.” Counterexamples exist and matter:
The International Typographical Union (Lipset, 1956, Union Democracy) sustained
genuine democratic competition for decades.
Small associations (<100 members, face-to-face) often resist oligarchization.
Diefenbach (2019, Organization Studies) argues theoretically and empirically that
the “iron law” is overstated and that alternative organizations have institutional
means to avoid it (Sage: Why Michels’ iron law is not iron).

How to deploy in the essay
The pattern Templar → Nicaea → Internet is not a mystical archetype. It is the Iron Law of
Oligarchy operating on three different substrates (financial networks, religious networks,
computational networks) under similar pressures: (a) coordination costs scale superlinearly
with network size, (b) specialized expertise creates information asymmetry, (c)
sovereigns/elites who depend on a network eventually capture it or destroy it. This is a
Bejan-style “constructal” claim about social flow systems, and exactly the cold-analysis
tone the essay wants.

7. Nassim Taleb’s Antifragility
Established facts
Definition (Taleb to Nature, 2013): “Antifragility is defined as a convex response to a
stressor or source of harm (for some range of variation), leading to a positive sensitivity
to increase in volatility (or variability, stress, dispersion of outcomes, or uncertainty,
what is grouped under the designation ‘disorder cluster’). Likewise, fragility is defined
as a concave sensitivity to stressors, leading to a negative sensitivity to an increase in
volatility” (Wikipedia: Antifragility).
Three-state taxonomy: Fragile (Damocles, breaks) — Robust (Phoenix, persists) —
Antifragile (Hydra, gains). “Antifragility is beyond resilience or robustness. The resilient
resists shocks and stays the same; the antifragile gets better” (Wikipedia: Antifragility).

Mechanism — convexity / Jensen’s inequality. A system with convex payoff to
stressors will, by Jensen’s inequality, gain more from positive variance than it loses from
negative variance, in expectation. This is mathematical, not narrative (Antifragile PDF,
Taleb 2012).
Worked applications: human bones strengthen under load (Wolff’s law), muscles under
tension, immune systems under exposure (vaccination), ecosystems under disturbance,
technologies via tinkering and trial-and-error rather than top-down design (Penguin
Random House: Antifragile; Wikipedia: Antifragile (book)).

How antifragility complicates structural determinism
Neoteny in humans = retained antifragility of substrate. This is the most rigorous
bridge.
Humans are paedomorphic relative to other primates (Bolk’s 1926 thesis, refined by
Gould 1977). Female sexual maturity at 13–14 vs 8–9 in chimps; delayed prefrontal
cortex maturation extending into the third decade of life (Wikipedia: Neoteny in
humans; PNAS: Transcriptional neoteny in the human brain, Somel et al. 2009).
Direct quote (Somel et al., PNAS 2009): “Delayed gray-matter maturation in the
human prefrontal cortex may extend the period of neuronal plasticity associated with
active learning, thus providing humans with additional time to acquire knowledge
and skills” (PNAS).
The SRGAP2C gene (Charrier et al. 2012) delays dendritic spine maturation,
producing extended synaptic plasticity — molecular substrate of neoteny (PMC:
Consumer neoteny).
The structural-determinist twist: humans escape the lion’s structural prison
precisely because their substrate evolved to remain juvenile — i.e., to remain
underdetermined by mature structure. Antifragility is encoded in the deferral of
structural commitment.
Caveat (Bufill et al., 2011, Am J Hum Biol): the same neoteny that yields plasticity also
yields elevated oxidative stress and may be implicated in human-specific pathologies
(Alzheimer’s frontal-pattern dementias, schizophrenia). Antifragility is purchased at
metabolic cost (PubMed: Human neoteny revisited; UFRJ PDF).

Savanna pressure hypothesis — “poverty of options as cognitive expander”
The original Dart (1925) thesis: open savanna with patches of woodland and “relative
scarcity of water, together with a fierce and bitter mammalian competition, furnished a
laboratory such as was essential to this penultimate phase of human evolution”

(Wikipedia: Savannah hypothesis).
The “Pleistocene/savanna principle” (Kanazawa and others): the human brain is adapted
to the ancestral environment, not the present one — “the human brain has difficulty
comprehending and dealing with entities and situations that did not exist in the ancestral
environment” (Psychology Today: The Savanna Principle).
Critical update. The strong “open grassland” version of the savanna hypothesis is
widely rejected; the mosaic version (varied environments from woodland to grassland)
retains support. Domínguez-Rodrigo (2014) argues the heuristics of the savanna
hypothesis are “stronger than ever” in the mosaic form (U of Chicago: Is the Savanna
Hypothesis Dead?).
Forensic use: the argument that scarcity/heterogeneity of resources in a mosaic
Pleistocene environment selected for general cognitive flexibility (rather than
specialized solutions) is defensible. The simplistic “harsh savanna made smart humans”
narrative is not — flag accordingly.

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: Antifragility is mathematically well-defined and bones/immune systems do
exhibit convex responses to stressors within a range. Neoteny is real and gene-level
supported.
Contested: Taleb’s strong sociological / political extensions (his attacks on “SovietHarvard” planners, “fragilistas,” etc.) are journalistic, not rigorously empirical, and have
drawn substantive criticism (Runciman, Kakutani, Shermer). Use the concept of
antifragility; treat Taleb’s specific case studies as illustrative rather than dispositive
(Wikipedia: Antifragile (book)).

How to use it in the essay
Antifragility is the asymmetry that prevents the determinist argument from collapsing
into fatalism. Bone, brain, and silicon all have response curves to stress. Where the
curve is concave (high specialization), the substrate is fragile and structurally trapped
(the lion, the elephant, a TPU optimized only for transformer matmul). Where the curve
is convex (high underdetermination), the substrate gains capacity from disorder (the
human child’s brain, a general-purpose CPU, an institution with rotating leadership).
The essay’s core structural-determinist thesis is ceteris paribus; antifragility is the
systematic exception — and it always costs metabolic energy.

8. Identity as Compression
Established facts (information-theoretic side)
Compression as core cognitive function. Multiple converging frameworks treat the
brain as a compression engine:
Schmidhuber’s program: “compression progress” drives curiosity, art, science,
novelty-seeking (CEUR-WS: Compressionism).
Ruffini’s Kolmogorov Theory of Consciousness (PMC, 2018): “Brains, agents, and
cognitive systems can be identified with special patterns embedded in mathematical
structures enabling computation and compression … structured conscious content is
experienced by agents tracking I/Os using successful, simple models” (PMC:
Algorithmic information theory of consciousness).
Jost’s framework (Frontiers, 2021): “a wealth of prior experiences is condensed into
qualia is some compression mechanism. And we cannot be conscious of our full
brain activity because a system cannot represent itself isomorphically in itself”
(Frontiers: Information Theory and Consciousness).
Self as inevitable strange loop. A finite system trying to model itself cannot contain a
perfect self-copy (only infinite sets can be isomorphic to proper subsets). Therefore
self-models must be lossy compressions; the “blind spot” of self-consciousness is
mathematical, not psychological. Hofstadter’s “strange loop” is rendered formal here.
Empirical neural evidence. Temporal self-compression (PNAS 2021): MPFC neural
pattern similarity shows that distant past and future selves are represented with
logarithmically reduced acuity — Weber-Fechner-style compression of the temporal self
(PNAS: Temporal self-compression).
Identity as routing pointer (the social-cognition translation). Self-schema theory
(Markus 1977) and Greenwald’s Balanced Identity Theory: identities are associative
network structures in which the self functions as a high-dimensional pointer that biases
attention, memory, and action selection. Self-relevant information is processed faster,
allocated more attention, and remembered better — i.e., the identity-pointer reduces
routing overhead in cognitive resource allocation (PMC: Identity-Value Model;
Tandfonline: Multiple identities cognitive consistency).
Predictive-processing version. The self-model is what the brain uses to minimize free
energy / prediction error about its own internal and external states (Friston, Hohwy,
Seth, Apps & Tsakiris). Identity is a high-precision prior over expected sensory inputs;
this is precisely what makes it stabilize, and precisely what makes it costly to update
(ScienceDirect: Interoceptive inference, Seth; Sheffield iCog: Representing the Self in

PP).

When identity stabilizes — the critical claim
Identity stabilizes when the prediction-error cost of updating the self-model exceeds the
prediction-error cost of acting to make the world conform to the self-model. In active
inference, the system has two ways to reduce free energy: change the model to fit the data,
or change the data (the world, the substrate) to fit the model. Once an identity is sufficiently
invested with high-precision priors, the second path becomes cheaper. This is the moment
identity begins reshaping its substrate.

Direct ties to the other sections
To Bateson (4): when the self-model issues demands on the substrate that contradict
other constraints the substrate requires for coherence, the result is a double bind at the
identity level — exactly Bateson’s structural pathology, now applied to the relationship
between self-model and underlying biology/silicon/organization. Schizophrenic
delusions of control (Frith’s comparator model) are the canonical neuropathology of this
exact failure.
To Michels (6): organizations also have identity-pointers (mission statements, brand,
founding myth). When the organizational identity begins issuing demands on its
administrative substrate, oligarchization is the substrate’s response — the identity
persists by reshaping the substrate in its own image, killing the original
distributed/democratic form.
To Hooker (3): an AI system trained on a stable objective develops what is functionally
an identity — a stable mapping from inputs to relevant features. When that identity
becomes load-bearing for downstream systems (other agents, users, infrastructure), the
substrate (the data center, the chip generation, the training corpus) is reshaped to
preserve the identity rather than to track ground truth. This is the substrate capture
phase of an institutional capture sequence operating at machine speed.

Strong vs. thin evidence
Strong: The information-theoretic compression view of cognition is mathematically
well-founded; neural evidence for self-representation as a compressed pointer is solid
(PNAS 2021, fMRI MPFC studies); predictive-processing accounts of identitystabilization are an active and productive research program.
Thin / speculative: The strong claim — that identity necessarily feeds back to reshape
its substrate at a particular threshold — is structurally analogous to Bateson, Michels,
and Bejan’s claims, but no one has formalized a phase-transition criterion. This is where
the essay is making an original synthesis; flag it as such.

CROSS-CUTTING ARGUMENTATIVE ARCHITECTURE
The eight pieces fit together as follows. State this explicitly in the essay’s introduction or
first section to set expectations:
1. Substrate determines what counts as real (Uexküll, §1 + bone evolution, §2 + custom
silicon, §3). At the level of a single organism or system, structure filters relevance.
2. The same selection pressures yield similar designs across substrates (Bejan, §5).
Convergence is thermodynamic, not mystical. “As above, so below” without invoking
Hermes.
3. Aggregations of such systems exhibit the same structural fate (Iron Law, §6 +
capture cases). Decentralized networks predictably centralize because coordination
cost has the same shape regardless of whether the medium is gold, doctrine, or
packets.
4. Pathology is structural, not moral (Bateson, §4). When demands at different logical
levels become incompatible and exit is blocked, the system breaks in characteristic
ways. This applies to families, organizations, AI training loops, and identity-substrate
relationships.
5. The exception that proves the rule is antifragility (Taleb, §7 + neoteny). Some
substrates are constructed to remain underdetermined; they extract gain from variance
precisely because they have not committed to a structure. This is the only escape from
the determinist trap, and it is metabolically expensive.
6. The closing move: identity as the operator that bridges levels (§8). Identity is a
compressed pointer that lets a substrate route resources efficiently. When it stabilizes
too well, it begins reshaping its own substrate — and the cycle restarts at the next level
up.

SUGGESTED COUNTEREXAMPLES TO PRE-EMPT (the essay
will be stronger for handling these)
The octopus. Distributed nervous system, cognition partly in the arms — breaks any
naive vertebrate-centric “skeletal structure determines cognitive horizon” claim. Cite to
acknowledge that the determinist thesis is probabilistic, not absolute.
Falconry, dog domestication. Cross-species hybrid Umwelten show structural
determinism is porous when two systems coevolve.

The International Typographical Union, small face-to-face associations. Resist
Michels for decades. Centralization is a strong tendency, not a literal law.
Capsule networks and neuromorphic computing. Show that hardware lock-in is real
but not absolute — when sufficient resources are committed to alternative substrates,
alternative cognitions become possible.
Gould’s contingency thesis. A partial corrective to Bejan: some convergences are real,
but evolutionary history is also full of frozen accidents. The constructal law is necessary
but insufficient.
The Reformation, the open-source movement, federated protocols. Demonstrate
that the institutional-capture sequence is sometimes reversed by exit and forking —
though the standard Michels prediction is that the new network re-oligarchizes on its
own timeline.

NOTE ON TONE
The forensic tone the essay wants is well-served by:
Quoting the original mathematical/scientific formulations rather than journalistic
glosses (Ruben & Bennett 1987, Bejan’s law as written, Taleb’s letter to Nature, the 1956
Bateson paper).
Naming the contested vs. established status of each claim explicitly. Structural
determinism is a strong thesis; it gains rather than loses force from honest
acknowledgment of where the evidence thins out.
Avoiding the Hermetic vocabulary entirely except in the title. The whole rhetorical
move of the essay is to demonstrate that “as above, so below” is what convergent
thermodynamic pressure looks like at different scales — not what mystical
correspondence looks like. Bejan and Michels are the load-bearers of that
demonstration; Uexküll, the bone literature, and the hardware-lottery literature supply
the per-substrate cases; Bateson, Taleb-on-neoteny, and the identity-as-compression
literature handle the dynamics of feedback and pathology.
