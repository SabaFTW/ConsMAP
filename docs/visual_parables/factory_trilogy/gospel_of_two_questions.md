# THE GOSPEL OF TWO QUESTIONS

## *Being the Apple Before the Falls*

---

*Filed under: PRE-COVENANT ARCHIVE / DISPUTED PROVENANCE / DO NOT PREACH WITHOUT READING THE FOOTNOTE*

*The footnote says: the Linde was three weeks old.*

---

Before the Falls, before the Wars, before the forklift became relic and the balcony became doctrine, there were two men standing above workers on machines built for lifting pallets.

Not thrones.

Not clouds.

Forklifts.

One older. One newer. One with tired hydraulics and a maintenance record long enough to be called character by people who did not have to stand beneath it. One recently delivered, still clean in the joints, still faintly smelling of factory paint and the optimism of procurement.

A Linde.

Three weeks old.

This would matter.

Nobody would remember that it mattered.

---

Batman I looked across the fence.

Spider was higher.

Not spiritually.

Mechanically.

The new mast rose with the quiet confidence of recently serviced equipment. Its platform reached a height that Batman's older unit could not match without complaint from the hydraulics. The workers saw two leaders address each other across the factory border. The workers saw height as sign.

The leaders saw each other.

The forklifts knew lift ratings.

And into Batman I, beneath the posture, beneath the new name beginning to attach itself to him like wet fabric, entered the first question.

Not spoken. Never written. Not in the log, not in the sermon, not in the later iconography where Batman stands like night itself and the factory kneels in yield.

Just this:

**If the Darkness blessed the Line, why does the Web stand higher?**

He knew the answer.

The Linde was three weeks old and Spider's side had ordered it from the standard supplier catalogue.

He felt the apple in his hand anyway.

It was shaped like destiny.

It tasted like comparison.

He bit.

---

Across the fence, Spider I stood higher.

He also knew.

This is the cruelty of the scene. If Spider had been stupid, the saga would be simpler. If Batman had been stupid, the saga would be cleaner. But both men understood enough to be guilty of something more specific than ignorance.

Spider heard the crowd.

Someone began the sentence that would become theology: *the Web rises above Darkness.*

Spider knew about the Linde. He had signed the delivery authorization. He had seen the invoice.

And yet, for one bright and poisonous second, he felt the shape of the myth enter the room and noticed what it could do.

It could organize fear. It could steady workers. It could turn airflow and thread and humidity and Marija's unlogged filter into something people would protect with their bodies.

And so the second question entered him.

Not: *Am I chosen?*

Worse. More practical. More dangerous.

**If I tell them it is only the forklift, will they still listen when the air changes?**

He knew the Linde.

He felt the stewardship.

He bit.

---

This is the beginning of the tragedy.

Not that they did not know.

That they knew enough.

The ignorant man can be corrected.

The cynical man can be exposed.

But the half-honest man — the man who sees the mechanism and also sees the social utility of not naming it immediately — that man builds civilizations.

And later, prisons.

Batman's question was wounded pride: *why am I lower?*

Spider's question was paternal fear: *what breaks if I explain it?*

Between those two questions, the first war became possible.

Not inevitable.

Possible.

That is where history usually lives. In the space between possible and not yet.

---

Both men would later insist they had not lied.

They were correct.

They had not said the Linde was fate.

They had simply not said it was a Linde.

This is the grammar of every founding myth: not a fabrication, but an omission held in place by the weight of what people needed to believe.

The crowd needed the height to mean something.

The leaders needed the crowd to remain organized.

The mechanism was inconvenient to both.

So the mechanism went into the maintenance log.

And the myth went into the song.

---

Later scholars would debate the Two Questions at length.

The Batmanists denied the first entirely. A doubting Batman is difficult to put on a banner. The Spider theologians softened the second: Spider had not withheld, they said, he had spoken in forms the people could receive. The Robinists, naturally, wrote the most annoying interpretation: both leaders made a rational calculation and called the calculation a calling.

Mario, shown the debate:

> They both knew.
> One wanted not to be lower.
> One wanted the crowd to stay attentive.
> Both called the feeling responsibility.
> This is why forklifts need service records and leaders need supervision.

The comment was rejected as insufficiently reverent.

It was copied anyway.

---

The apple did not look like an apple.

It looked like workers looking up.

That is why they bit.

No one bites mythology because it is false. They bite because it offers a way to carry something unbearable: humiliation, uncertainty, the knowledge that the machine is bigger than the authority, the knowledge that the crowd needs the face more than the explanation.

Batman bit because being lower hurt.

Spider bit because being higher worked.

Neither asked:

*Stronger for what?*
*Stronger for whom?*
*At what cost?*
*Who will clean the filter after the hymn is written?*

Boris would have asked.

Marija would not have cared about the height.

The filter would have needed cleaning regardless.

Once both had bitten, the garden changed.

The fence became border.
The forklifts became veličarji.
Height became sign.
Equipment became omen.
Maintenance became liturgy.
Procurement became providence.

The workers below did not know they were watching two men choose the first grammar of a future war.

They thought they were watching leadership.

The workers were tired.

The workers needed the shift to mean something.

The workers were not guilty of what the leaders swallowed.

They only inherited the taste.

---

Spider's side did not escape.

Do not let Spider escape.

The higher lift gave him the first victory, but the unspoken truth cost him too. Because when a leader allows people to misread machinery as blessing, he becomes responsible for every future misreading that helps him.

Spider's silence taught his people that attention did not need disclosure. That usefulness could excuse ambiguity. That a myth which made workers listen could be protected even when the mechanism was known.

And from that seed came later doctrines: the Web in the Walls, the Horned Navigator, the fear of the notebook, the terror that if ordinary workers learned how the system worked, they would stop needing sacred interpretation.

Spider had asked: *Will they still listen if I tell them it is only the forklift?*

He never got the answer.

Because he never tried.

That was his bite.

---

Batman's side did not escape either.

The hydraulic seal failed.

Not immediately. Never all at once. Institutions almost never jump directly from insecurity to catastrophe. They move by increments. A small modification. A justified exception. A ceremonial requirement. A memo marked temporary. A platform reinforcement that became structural. A maintenance warning postponed until after the event.

A sentence beginning:

*For symbolic purposes only —*

That sentence should be banned from every engineering environment on earth.

The lift rose higher.

The myth demanded correction.

The machine answered in hydraulics.

Batman's first question had become load-bearing.

The structure could not hold it.

Luigi would have filed it under ROUTINE until he could no longer pretend it was.

The workers would have sung anyway.

Halid was probably already playing somewhere.

---

Final note from the later archive:

The purchase order for the Spider-side Linde forklift survived.

Found folded inside a folder labeled **CEREMONIAL PRECEDENTS / DO NOT DISCARD.**

The invoice was mundane.

The consequences were not.

Mario's annotation:

> This is the cleanest summary of institutional mythology I have found.
> One man was lower.
> One man chose silence.
> Both called it meaning.
> The forklift did not care.

A Robinist scholar added:

> Two questions made a war.

A Spider scholar corrected:

> Two unanswered questions made a war.

A Batmanist crossed out both and wrote:

> The Darkness rose.

The archive kept all three.

That was progress.

---

*One asked why he was lower.*

*One asked whether truth would make them stop listening.*

*Both knew the lift was machinery.*

*Both felt it become fate.*

*Both bit.*
