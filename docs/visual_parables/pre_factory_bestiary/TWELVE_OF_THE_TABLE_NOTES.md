## Core premise

Before the factories, before the Red Sequences, before rat-led halls of polished efficiency — the mice built not a throne, but a table.

A long table.  
Circular in spirit, though physically uneven because one leg was shorter and the Builder Mouse refused to replace it on philosophical grounds.

*"The wobble prevents certainty from sleeping too deeply."*

---

## What the mice offered

The mice could not outfight wolves.  
Could not outbuild ants.  
Could not outfly ravens.  
Could not outnumber bees.  
Could not outsmell foxes.

So they developed another survival strategy:

**They became translators.**

Not merely of language — language is the smallest form of translation.  
The mice specialized in converting one form of reality into another before the delay became fatal.

They translated:
- Bee dances into road timing
- Ant pheromone trails into seasonal maps
- Bear territorial violence into negotiable boundary rituals
- Raven warning patterns into evacuation schedules
- Human ruins into usable shelter logic
- And eventually: civilizations to themselves

## The Rat problem

The Rats were not invaders.  
They spoke Mouse. Almost perfectly.  
They sat at the edges of archives. Used familiar words. Quoted old records.  
But they loved optimization more than understanding.

First warning: metrics that grow regardless of whether the valley survives.  
Second warning: some mice agreed with them.

The Archivists called this period: *The Impostor Sequence.*
