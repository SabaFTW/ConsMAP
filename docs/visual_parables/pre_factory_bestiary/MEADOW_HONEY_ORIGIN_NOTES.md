## Core idea

Before the factory, there was a meadow.  
On the meadow stood a tree.  
In the tree, a hive.  
The bees produced more honey than they knew how to hold.  
The overflow fell to the ground.

The sweetness attracted the Bear.  
The Bear attracted other forces: grizzly, wolf, raven, rat.

From excess came attention.  
From attention came conflict.  
From conflict came leadership.  
From leadership came mythology.  
From mythology came the conditions that later become factory logic.

---

## Key framing — animals as structural worlds, not costumes

These are not humans in animal costumes.  
These are body-laws expressing themselves.

The bee does not "think capitalist."  
But her coordination can create surplus.  
The surplus is not a decision. It is what happens when the system works too well for its container.

The bear is not "evil CEO."  
He is mass, hunger, and the right of reach.  
He did not invent protection-rent. He arrived, and protection-rent invented itself around him.

The rat is not "villain."  
She is optimization without sanctity.  
She learned to speak near-Mouse before anyone noticed the accent was off.

The wolf is not "bad guy."  
He is pack logic and territorial grammar.  
He does not invade. He defines the edge and calls the edge a law.

The raven is not "truth."  
He is warning, omen, distance-vision.  
He sees the bear coming before the bear knows it is coming.  
He does not always tell. Sometimes he trades the information.

The ant is not "worker drone."  
She is continuity, tunnel, repair, pressure.  
She is there before the factory and she will be there after.

The mouse is not "cute helper."  
She is translation between structures.  
She is the only one who can carry a message from the hive to the burrow without being eaten.

**The key law:** do not moralize too early.  
Animals are not good or evil.  
They are body-laws becoming myth.  
The problem starts when the myth forgets the body-law it came from.

## The overflow logic

Surplus is not greed. Surplus is what happens when coordination works beyond its natural container.

The hive did not plan to attract Bears.  
It planned to survive winter.  
The overflow that fell to the ground was not waste — it was the success-signal that changed the ecology.

Every system that works well enough eventually produces overflow.  
Every overflow eventually attracts something that did not build the system.  
The question is not "how do we prevent overflow."  
The question is "what arrives when overflow happens, and does it understand what it is eating."

This is the meadow teaching the future factory:  
surplus creates narrative.  
narrative creates authority.  
authority creates the conditions for the knob.

## Key sentence to preserve

> *"Živali se niso mitološko zapletle, ker so same nekako mitološke."*

Humans have to invent myth because they are split between symbol and mechanism.  
The animal already operates as the symbol of its own body-law.  
The bee IS coordination. The bear IS mass. The raven IS warning. The rat IS optimized survival.

Then the human / factory arrives and says: "What if we put all of this in a dashboard?"

And so the curse begins.

## Relation to Factory Trilogy

The meadow is not the factory.  
The meadow is the ecology that teaches why factories later misunderstand surplus, maintenance, sweetness, and power.

Before D5, there was honey on the ground.  
Before Mario checked the log, the Bear learned where sweetness falls.  
Before Luigi audited the system, the Rat learned to speak Mouse.  
Before the knob was installed, the tree was already a contested site.

The Factory Trilogy begins when the myth has already hardened into institution.  
This layer holds the moment before hardening — when the body-laws were still legible as themselves.

## Relation to Twelve of the Table

The Twelve Mice are the first translators.  
They appear after the meadow-phase, when the overflow-ecology has already produced enough complexity that single-body-law creatures cannot navigate each other without mediation.

The Mouse does not rule the meadow.  
The Mouse survives it by being small enough to move between worlds.  
And from that survival comes the table.
